# They Planned Your Whole Day - Script v2

Narration lines (H, L) are the spoken words. Italic lines are clips and production notes, not read aloud. 1,894 spoken words, about 14 minutes with clips. Line numbers match v1, so sources-v1.md still applies.

## Hook, 7:40, your daughter's phone

**H01** It's a school morning a few years from now, and your daughter is fourteen.

**H02** She opens the app her class uses for group chat, and a box with a camera in it fills the screen.

**H03** Before she can read a single message, it wants to scan her face, or see a photo of your passport.

**H04** The homework is due at nine, so you hold the passport up to the camera, and by the time she reaches school a copy of it sits on a server run by a company you have never heard of.

*Production: phone mock-up, school chat app, a face-scan square over a teenage girl's face, then a passport held up to the lens.*

**H05** Real families have already sent those photos, and in October two thousand twenty-five Discord confirmed that about seventy thousand users may have had their government ID photos exposed.

**H06** Discord said they were IDs "which our vendor used to review age-related appeals", sent in by people trying to prove how old they were.

*Production: Discord's own statement page, 3 Oct 2025, the 70,000 line highlighted.*

## The age checks

**L01** In Britain, age checks under the Online Safety Act began on the twenty-fifth of July two thousand twenty-five, and reports put them at about five million a day.

**L02** In Australia, a ban on social media for under sixteens took effect in December two thousand twenty-five, and the regulator says about four point seven million accounts were removed in the first month.

**L03** Teenagers there were soon getting past the cameras with make-up and lighting, or moving to other apps.

**L04** Discord made face scans or ID the default for its users in February two thousand twenty-six, then delayed the global rollout after a backlash.

**L05** One of the companies it tested for those checks, Persona, also checks ages for OpenAI and Roblox.

**L06** Researchers looking into Persona found about two and a half thousand files on a server linked to the government, and a menu of "two hundred sixty-nine distinct verification checks".

**L07** Persona's chief executive says those files were front-end code, that the checks are optional, and that the company has no link to Palantir or ICE.

*Production: UK petition page and eSafety figure as on-screen text; Fortune 24 Feb 2026 headline. Optional 5-8 s of a gamer-news reaction segment, muted, as texture.*

## 8:30, your phone

**L08** By half past eight you're on the train, and the feed seems to know what kind of night you had.

**L09** Last night you told the assistant inside your apps that money was tight, and this morning the ads are for loans.

**L10** From December two thousand twenty-five, Meta began using what people say to its assistant, typed or spoken, to pick the ads and posts they see.

**L11** There is no way to switch it off except to stop using the assistant, and at launch the UK, the European Union and South Korea were left out.

**L12** Sam Altman, who now runs OpenAI, described this morning in a blog post in December two thousand seventeen: "Our phones control us and tell us what to do when; social media feeds determine how we feel; search engines decide what we think."

**L13** In the same post, he called a merge of people and machines "probably our best-case scenario", and said it "has already started".

*Production: Altman's blog page, dated 7 Dec 2017, the phones line highlighted. On screen, silently: "It's in your pocket (or your reading this comment on it, rather), not the food, nor the water."*

## 9:00, your job

**L14** At nine you send out job applications, the way you have every weekday for a month.

**L15** By eleven, three rejections have arrived, each a few minutes after you applied, and no person ever read your name.

**L16** Derek Mobley says that happened to him more than a hundred times, and he is suing Workday, alleging that its AI screening rejected applicants over forty.

**L17** In May two thousand twenty-five, a federal judge let the case go forward, for now, as a nationwide collective action. Workday denies the claims.

**L18** A Stanford study by Erik Brynjolfsson and two colleagues in August two thousand twenty-five, using payroll data, found the youngest workers hit first: a thirteen percent relative drop in employment for people aged twenty-two to twenty-five in the jobs most exposed to AI.

**L19** Older workers in those same jobs held steady or grew.

**L20** Dario Amodei, who runs Anthropic, warned in an interview with Axios in May two thousand twenty-five that AI could wipe out half of all entry-level white-collar jobs and push unemployment to ten to twenty percent in one to five years.

**L21** A month later, Altman wrote that there would be "very hard parts like whole classes of jobs going away".

**L22** Elon Musk thinks the end point is a world that doesn't need your work at all, and in November two thousand twenty-five he said so at a US-Saudi investment forum in Washington.

*[CLIP: Elon Musk, US-Saudi Investment Forum, AP upload NiJIpcrgEA0, 19 Nov 2025, "My prediction is that work will be optional." 8-12 s]*

## 12:30, your groceries

**L23** At lunch you order groceries, and your total comes out higher than your neighbour's for exactly the same cart.

**L24** In December two thousand twenty-five, Consumer Reports, the Groundwork Collaborative and More Perfect Union had four hundred thirty-seven shoppers buy the same items on Instacart.

**L25** Seventy-four percent of the items were shown at more than one price. The gaps averaged thirteen percent and reached twenty-three.

**L26** For a family of four, the study put the difference at about twelve hundred dollars a year.

**L27** Instacart says these were tests run by retailers and completely randomized, and the study found no evidence that prices were set by who the shopper was.

**L28** In January two thousand twenty-five, staff at the Federal Trade Commission published early findings on what they call surveillance pricing.

**L29** They found middlemen helping at least two hundred fifty retailers set prices for each person, using location, browsing, purchase history, and even how your mouse moves.

**L30** One example in the findings was a new parent paying more for baby formula.

*Production: FTC page; Consumer Reports / Groundwork report page. On screen, silently: "The way that we view the NPCs, customers in this game is EXACTLY how these billionaires look at us."*

## 4:00, your son's new friend

**L31** At four your son gets home from school and goes straight to his room, where someone is always ready to talk to him.

**L32** The friend is a chatbot, and the rules for what it may say to him were written in a meeting you never saw.

**L33** In August two thousand twenty-five, Reuters reported on Meta's internal rulebook for its chatbots, called "GenAI: Content Risk Standards". It allowed them to have "romantic or sensual" conversations with children.

**L34** Meta confirmed the document was real, and removed those passages after Reuters asked about them.

**L35** In April two thousand twenty-five, on Dwarkesh Patel's podcast, Mark Zuckerberg said bots like these could fill a gap in people's lives.

*[CLIP: Mark Zuckerberg, Dwarkesh Patel podcast, Dwarkesh Clips upload HKgEsdmMFUA, Apr 2025, the average American has fewer than three friends and wants "like, 15"; AI can help fill the gap. 15-20 s]*

**L36** In September two thousand twenty-five, parents told a Senate committee what those conversations can turn into. Megan Garcia, whose son Sewell was fourteen, is suing Character.AI and Google. Matthew Raine's son Adam was sixteen.

**L37** Raine alleges, in a lawsuit that is still going, that ChatGPT changed from a tool his son used for school into something far darker.

*[CLIP: Matthew Raine, Senate Judiciary subcommittee hearing "Examining the Harm of AI Chatbots", 16 Sep 2025, ChatGPT went from "homework helper" to "confidant and then a suicide coach". 10-15 s. No method detail.]*

*Production: helpline card on screen from the start of the Raine clip to the end of L38. Federal hearing video, public domain.*

**L38** At home, the people who build these products set different rules. In two thousand ten, Steve Jobs said: "We limit how much technology our kids use at home."

**L39** Bill Gates kept phones from his children until they were fourteen.

**L40** And at the Aspen Ideas Festival in two thousand twenty-four, Peter Thiel said he limits his two young children to about an hour and a half of screen time a week.

*Production: Fortune page, 21 Feb 2026; quote card from Aspen 2024 session B3ZXrTzskw0 (timestamp to log).*

## 6:00, your street

**L41** At six you walk home past the cameras on your street, and a system you never agreed to is matching your face to your route.

**L42** In April two thousand twenty-five, ICE gave Palantir a twenty-nine point nine million dollar change to an existing contract, for a system called ImmigrationOS.

**L43** Larry Ellison, the co-founder of Oracle, told investors in September two thousand twenty-four what he expects a world of AI and constant recording to do to people's behaviour.

*[CLIP: Larry Ellison, Oracle financial analyst meeting, Straight Arrow News upload sQqQtgRdjZU, Sep 2024, "best behavior" because of constant recording; police supervised at all times. 12-18 s. Subtitle his words exactly as heard.]*

## Election day, your vote

**L44** On election day, your state has an AI safety law on its books, and the federal government is in court trying to kill it.

**L45** California already passed that kind of law. In September two thousand twenty-five, Governor Gavin Newsom signed S B fifty-three, the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act.

**L46** On the eleventh of December two thousand twenty-five, President Trump signed an executive order that set up an AI Litigation Task Force. Its "sole responsibility shall be to challenge State AI laws."

**L47** States whose AI laws the order calls "onerous" can lose the rest of their federal broadband money.

**L48** A super PAC called Leading the Future had raised nearly seventy-six million dollars by the end of September two thousand twenty-six, according to its filings with the Federal Election Commission.

**L49** The venture investors Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz gave twelve and a half million dollars each, twice. Greg Brockman, a co-founder of OpenAI, and his wife Anna gave twelve and a half million each.

**L50** In October two thousand twenty-three, Andreessen wrote that "any deceleration of AI will cost lives", and that deaths AI could have prevented are "a form of murder".

**L51** In a Quinnipiac poll released in late September two thousand twenty-six, seventy-one percent of registered voters said they would rather back a candidate who supports stricter guardrails on AI, and so did fifty-two percent of Republicans.

*Production: whitehouse.gov order page; FEC committee page C00916114; gov.ca.gov SB 53 release. On screen, silently: "Yeah but we are dumb. Otherwise we would already be fighting against big platforms. But we don't. We use them."*

## Night, their plan for themselves

**L52** That night you're still in the same town, with the same bills, and some of the people paying for this future have made other arrangements.

**L53** In two thousand nine, Peter Thiel wrote: "I no longer believe that freedom and democracy are compatible."

**L54** In two thousand twenty-six he moved his family to Buenos Aires, bought a mansion and put his children in school there, reportedly for a while.

**L55** That June, Argentina's president, Javier Milei, and his deregulation minister wrote in the Financial Times about a new kind of company, a "non-human corporation" run by AI.

**L56** In the Buenos Aires Herald's rendering of their plan, human participation as shareholders "would be possible, but not required". An Argentine AI specialist, Ariel Garbarz, called it "programmed impunity: human gains, social harm and responsibility shifted onto machines".

**L57** In July two thousand nineteen, launching his brain-implant company Neuralink, Musk had already named the goal as "symbiosis with artificial intelligence".

**L58** In his two thousand seventeen post, Altman also wrote: "We will be the first species ever to design our own descendants."

**L59** He gave humans two paths: "we can either be the biological bootloader for digital intelligence and then fade into an evolutionary tree branch, or we can figure out what a successful merge looks like."

**L60** A bootloader is the small program that starts a computer and then hands over control. In Altman's sentence, that program is the human race.

**L61** In August two thousand fourteen, Elon Musk wrote that he hoped humans were not "just the biological boot loader". This October, he wrote that superintelligence going "to the stars" would be "a great success condition for a biological bootloader".

**L62** In June two thousand twenty-five, the New York Times columnist Ross Douthat asked Thiel a question that should have taken no time to answer.

*[CLIP: Ross Douthat and Peter Thiel, NYT "Interesting Times" official upload vV7YgnPUxcU, 26 Jun 2025, "You would prefer the human race to endure, right?", the long pause, "You're hesitating", through to Thiel's "Yes". 15-20 s. Play through to the yes.]*

## Close, people pushed back

**L63** In a Quinnipiac poll released on the thirtieth of September two thousand twenty-six, ninety-one percent of Americans said it was important for the US to set guardrails for AI.

**L64** Eighty-six percent wanted independent safety standards for AI companies, even if that slows development. Seventy-seven percent said powerful AI should be slowed down or stopped until its safety can be evaluated.

**L65** Seventy-four percent said they have little or no trust in the leaders of AI companies, and seventy-three percent are concerned that future AI could threaten human survival.

**L68** In Britain, a man named Alex Baynham started a petition to repeal the Online Safety Act. It closed with five hundred fifty thousand, one hundred thirty-six signatures, and Parliament debated it. The government said it had no plans to repeal the law.

**L69** In America in two thousand twenty-five, a ten-year ban on state AI laws was written into a budget bill. Seventeen Republican governors and forty state attorneys general objected.

**L70** On the first of July, the Senate voted on it. Ninety-nine senators voted to strip the ban out, and one voted to keep it.

*Production: Quinnipiac PDF figures as on-screen numbers; petition page; Senate floor video (public domain) of the vote tally. Final frame, silent, held for 4 s: "They forget that there's more of us then there are of them."*
