L20At nine o'clock, in four states, pharmacies open and their screens are blank.

L21The company that built the agent can switch it off. In every real case so far, that is what happened. The question this draft answers is what happens when the company does not.

L22The draft covers only three kinds of danger: a biological weapon, a cyberattack, or a model escaping its makers' control. A chatbot that talks a teenager into dying is not on the list.

L23Under the draft, as reported, an independent evaluator must first find what the text calls an imminent enumerated risk. The evaluator then has seventy-two hours, counted from the day it finishes its assessment, to tell the Commerce Department.

L24Commerce then goes to a federal judge in Washington and asks for a suspension.

L25No other department, agency or officer of the United States may suspend the model. One critic who read the draft, Nathan Calvin of the group Encode, says it would also stop state courts from doing it.

L26And the evaluator can be paid by the company it is evaluating, as long as the pay is not tied to what it finds.
