# Visual bible: "the threshold" film

The rule that decides everything: nothing on screen is drawn. Every frame is a real photograph, a real document, a real clip or real stock footage, moved and graded. The motion comes from depth, camera and type, never from illustration.

## The look in one line

Cold near-black rooms, real people cut out and standing in them, a slow camera that never stops moving, one red number that keeps climbing.

## Palette

| Role | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base | #08090b to #15181d | Every background. Never pure black, never grey-blue. |
| Ink | #ebe8e1 | Headlines and names. Warm white, never pure white. |
| Ink 2 | #8f929b | Labels, dates, roles. |
| Red | #c41e3a | The accent. Thin rules, the count, one word at a time. Never a full plate, never a background. |
| Paper | #ece6d8 | Document cards and logo tags. |

Plates (the photo or footage behind a scene) are graded cold: saturation down to about 35 percent, shadows lifted slightly blue, highlights neutral. Subjects (cutouts, documents) keep about 70 percent saturation so skin and paper read warm against the cold room. The red is the only saturated colour in the frame, which is why one small red number carries so much weight.

## Depth: how every scene is built

Five planes, far to near:
1. **Plate.** A photo or B-roll clip, blurred 6-12 px, at 35-55 percent opacity, slow push.
2. **Light.** A soft shaft from one side, screen blend, drifting.
3. **Dust.** Fine motes at low opacity, rising slowly. Never snow.
4. **Subject.** A cutout person, a document card, a clip window. Sharp, with a soft drop shadow. This plane moves most.
5. **Type.** Labels and the count, with a soft shadow so they sit on anything.

The camera pushes in 4-10 percent over a shot and drifts by a few pixels on a slow sine, like a handheld long lens. Each plane moves by its depth, so the frame separates and breathes. Nothing is static for longer than a second.

## Treatments, by material

**People.** Cut out from Commons photos with a segmentation model, edges and hair kept. Placed on the subject plane, lit from the shaft side, slightly desaturated, 1.5 px blur on anyone behind the main subject. Name and role in small spaced caps, bottom left, with a 3 px red rule. They enter by sliding a few pixels and fading, never by popping.

**Documents.** The real page (the bill as reported, the statute, the study PDF, the court filing), on a paper card with a soft shadow, pushed in slowly. The line that matters gets a red underline that draws left to right, or a highlight box. Names and numbers that must be hidden get a black redaction bar that slides on. Pages can stack: a new page slides in on top, tilted 2-3 degrees, the one below dims.

**Logos.** On small paper tags, dropped in one by one with a tiny bounce, never floating on black.

**News clips.** In a framed window on the subject plane (thin warm-white border, 2 px), with the outlet and date in small caps above a 3 px red rule. The plate behind is a related photo, blurred and cold. The window can be full frame for a strong line, or two-thirds with the count beside it.

**Stock B-roll.** Treated as plates, never as the subject. Cold grade, blurred when a subject is in front, sharp when it carries the beat alone (a server room, a trading floor, an empty office).

**Screenshots and posts.** Real posts appear on screen silently, as screenshots on cards, never read by the narrator.

## The count

The film's spine is a counter in the top right: a small red number, tabular figures, with "DEATHS" or "DAMAGE" in spaced caps beneath. It appears when the scenario starts and never leaves the frame until the scenario ends. It ticks on cuts, not continuously. Beside it, a second small counter in ink-2: the threshold, "50", which never changes. The gap between them is the tension.

## Type

Inter Display. Headlines 60-96 px, weight 700, tight tracking. Labels 22 px, weight 600, 5 px tracking, caps. One headline at most on screen, and only when the narrator is silent or the line is a number. Type appears by typing on (0.6-1.0 s) or rising 20 px while fading. Underlines draw from the left. Nothing bounces.

## Rhythm

- A new visual event every 3-4 seconds in the first two minutes: a cut, a reveal, a count tick, a document line underlined, a cutout stepping forward.
- The narrator states the point, then the clip confirms it. The clip's first frame lands within half a second of the narrator finishing.
- Chapter changes are a hard cut to black for six frames, then the next plate fades up over 20 frames.
- Shakes are rare and short (a quarter second, 4-8 px) and only on a word that earns one.

## Sound-picture sync

Every Production line in the script names the frame at the moment the narrator says the key word, so the edit can be built from the script alone. Clip markers give the in and out words.

## Texture

Film grain at 9-11 percent, overlay blend, moving every frame. Vignette to 78 percent at the corners. No scan lines, no glitches, no light leaks except a slow one under the hook.

## The hook, 0:00-0:30

Starts on a dated, concrete frame: a real page with a real number on it. The count appears inside 10 seconds. By 0:30 the viewer has seen the document, the number, a face and a clip. No logo sting, no channel intro, no title card until after the first reveal.

## What never appears

Drawn icons, flat vector shapes, stock "AI brain" imagery, glowing circuit boards, robot hands, matrix rain, red duotone plates, text that bounces, more than one headline at a time, any frame that is still.
